# TikTok

Make gaming content that feels made by a gamer, not a generic AI content factory. This skill captures the channel's working preferences, not a universal formula for TikTok reach. The owner’s latest direction wins over examples and production defaults below.

## Approval is a separate stage

**Before publishing any post, show Renato the complete final post text and wait for his explicit approval. Never publish without that review.**

- Approval to create a video, liking the edit, or saying “post it” before seeing its text does not replace text review. Show the exact caption, line breaks, credits, and hashtags.
- Associate approval with a specific export, cover, account, and complete text. If any material element changes, show the changed result before publishing. Do not silently add credits, hashtags, wording, or AI labels after approval.
- This skill grants no permission to post, follow accounts, comment, delete, purchase, or change account settings. Prepare only what the user requested; external actions need their own authorization.
- Preserve the published item if a correction is requested. Do not delete/repost automatically or retry an uncertain submission.

## Start with the reference and the latest approved version

Resolve the actual reference file, current export, and requested change before editing. Inspect the media, not just its filename or an old recap. When the user asks for “the same vibe”, analyze the narration, phrasing, cuts, typeface, word timing, motion, visual framing, and audio separately.

Distinguish three formats:

- **Narrated gaming video:** one clear premise, concrete game footage, synchronized animated subtitles, a designed cover.
- **Meme:** preserve the supplied joke, clip, audio, and timing unless the requested change calls for replacing them. A request for “our version” is not a request to invent an unrelated animation. Adapt PT-BR text and the specific requested visual.
- **Photo post:** readable stills with a coherent comparison/story. Match units and time windows (e.g. three days versus three days), and verify claims before putting them on an image.

Keep the approved export and editable sources. Version new rounds instead of overwriting the only good copy. Record the font, sizes, positions, timings, source clips, narration settings, and cover selection in the project so the next video can genuinely reuse them.

## Editorial and editing style

- Write natural PT-BR. Start with the actual interesting fact or joke; avoid “neste vídeo”, generic hype, long setup, and unsupported superlatives.
- Verify game identity, footage provenance, release status, outfit names, references, prices, and numerical claims where relevant. Do not present a mod, fan concept, leak, or another game's footage as official gameplay.
- Let the footage prove the spoken claim. For an outfit showcase, show a short look at the outfit and then combat **wearing that outfit**, rather than holding a rotating 3D model for most of the segment. If matching footage cannot be sourced, say so instead of substituting misleading clips.
- Keep useful edits: a criticism that an effect looks bad calls for improving it, not stripping all effects. Use callouts, circles, zooms, and transitions only when they clarify the moment.
- Avoid arbitrary cutout pictures over gameplay, distracting duplicates, and cropped overlays. Fill the vertical frame intentionally, not with a top-heavy composition and a large unused bottom area.
- A short video should have a smaller premise, not merely faster narration of a long script. Let the payoff land; avoid a long branded outro.

## Titles and subtitles

The visual standard is **large, readable subtitles with a spoken-word swell/pop**, and **titles slightly smaller than the subtitles**. See [references/production.md](references/production.md) when building or revising the render.

- Use the approved reference’s font and animation when they are available. Do not invent a font name or claim an exact match from memory. If the font cannot be identified, choose a close licensed alternative and show a sample.
- Reveal/highlight in sync with speech. The active word should grow and settle smoothly, like a small elastic ripple, rather than making the entire sentence bounce. Keep glyphs crisp and lines stable.
- Keep titles at a consistent relative size across videos. A previous “increase 20%” was a correction to one export, not an instruction to enlarge every new version by another 20%.
- Do not pin titles against the top edge or add an unrelated footer. Keep text out of TikTok’s controls and description region, and check the actual mobile preview. Safe areas are a layout check, not a single permanent set of coordinates.
- For memes with a black text band, center the text vertically **within that band**, not within the whole video. Preserve comfortable padding and leave the joke’s subjects visible.

## Sound and narration

- Reuse the approved ElevenLabs voice/settings for narrated videos when available; do not substitute a different voice silently. Keep pronunciation natural, and listen to game names before rendering the full sequence.
- Keep source audio for memes unless asked to replace it. Do not add unrelated music during upload.
- The preferred ElevenLabs credit is a separate description line: `Narração com IA: elevenlabs.io`. Apply it only when that service was used. Do not add the voice's name or the credit to every meme/photo post.
- Confirm current voice usage rights and attribution requirements for the actual plan and intended use. Attribution is not a substitute for a commercial license.
- AI disclosure is a distinct decision from provider credit. Check current platform requirements for the actual content. Do not treat “credit in the description” as a universal substitute for a required label, and do not silently toggle a label the owner has not reviewed. Resolve any conflict before submission.

## Covers

Every video needs a deliberate cover, including short videos. Keep shared typography/readability, but **do not make all covers the same**.

Choose a composition around that video's subject: a recognizable face, outfit, action, or object, a short readable hook, contrast, and uncluttered framing. Check it both full size and cropped in the profile grid; it must read as a thumbnail. Do not add the channel logo unless asked. A previous “good enough for this first one” is not the permanent design ceiling.

Deliver the cover separately and verify that it is actually selected during upload. If the upload surface only supports a frame from the video, prepare and review a compatible cover frame; don't claim a separate cover was applied when it wasn't.

## Description, not burned-in subtitles

Use short human copy, real line breaks, and a compact set of relevant hashtags. Credits get their own paragraph. Avoid literal `\n`, a wall of tags, or an added sales pitch. `#meme` fits a meme; `#fyp` and `#viral` may be used when requested, but are not promises or fixes for distribution.

Example for narrated content (adapt to what the video actually shows):

```text
Qual dessas roupas do Wolverine tá mais irada? 🔥

Narração com IA: elevenlabs.io

#Wolverine #games
```

Example for the PS5 meme (no narration credit when keeping original audio):

```text
Eu e meu PS5 quando finalmente chega o fim de semana 😂🎮

#meme #ps5 #games #fyp #viral
```

Show the final text as a copyable block/message for approval; do not send these examples blindly.

## QA and handoff

1. Inspect scene boundaries, subtitle entrances and largest animated words, title placement, first/last frame, and cover crops. Check the subjects in every layout, not just an attractive still.
2. Watch the final export end to end with sound when the tools allow it. Check for silent tails, missing narration, clipped words, bad joins, drifting subtitles, and flickering masks. A prior export lost sound after 0:34: always check through the end.
3. Use technical checks as supporting evidence: duration, dimensions, audio streams, decode errors, narration alignment, and unexpected silence. An audio hash proves preservation, not that the sound is good. A contact sheet does not prove motion is smooth.
4. If continuous playback or listening is unavailable, say exactly what was and was not checked. Deliver it as a preview, not “fully verified”.
5. Hand over the versioned video, cover, final description, and concise change list. Keep previews easy to open on the user's requested device; a local path alone may not help someone on a phone.

## Publishing an approved post

Use the available supported connector or the user's authorized browser session. Confirm the intended account from the UI rather than assuming a past handle is still correct.

Before submitting, verify the exact file/version, cover, approved text with actual line breaks, visibility, sound, and disclosure settings. If the UI introduces a material change, stop before posting and explain it.

Submit once, then verify the resulting post and its URL/status. **“Upload succeeded” is not the same as “public”.** TikTok Studio has shown “Content under review” and “Only me” after an Everyone submission. Report the observed status honestly; do not repost or change privacy as a workaround. Check for an existing post before any retry after uncertainty.

When discussing reach, separate observations from hypotheses. A few views or AI narration alone does not establish a restriction or “shadowban”. Check actual privacy, processing/review state, recommendation eligibility, and available analytics before diagnosing distribution. Do not manufacture engagement or promise that following accounts/hashtags will unlock the algorithm.
